# Course Information

The course Semiconductor Physics and Basic Electronic Circuits provides fundamental knowledge of semiconductor materials, electronic devices, and basic electrical circuit analysis. The course covers the concepts of intrinsic and extrinsic semiconductors, energy band theory, and charge carrier conduction. It includes detailed study of the PN junction diode, its formation, biasing, V-I characteristics, and applications, along with the Zener diode, Zener and avalanche breakdown mechanisms, and voltage regulation applications. The course also explains different types of rectifiers, including half-wave, full-wave, center-tapped, and bridge rectifiers, along with ripple factor and filter circuits. In the transistor section, students learn the construction and operation of Bipolar Junction Transistors (BJT), including NPN and PNP transistors, transistor configurations such as CB, CE, and CC, and applications of transistors as amplifiers and switches. Basic circuit analysis techniques using Kirchhoff’s Current Law (KCL) and Kirchhoff’s Voltage Law (KVL) are introduced for solving electrical networks. The course further includes the study of transformers, covering construction, working principle, EMF equation, transformer losses, efficiency, and practical applications. Overall, the course develops theoretical understanding and practical skills in semiconductor devices and basic electronic circuits essential for electrical and electronics engineering students.
