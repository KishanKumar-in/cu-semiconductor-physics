Charge carriers, Majority and Minority Charge carriers, Basic Structure of P-N Junction diode:

Charge carriers are particles that carry electric current in a semiconductor.

## Two Types of Charge Carriers:

Electrons:

Negative charge (−)

Move freely in the conduction band

Holes:

Represent the absence of an electron in the valence band

Act like positive charges (+)

Electrons move into holes, making holes appear to move

## Majority and Minority Charge Carriers

In extrinsic semiconductors, due to doping, one type of charge carrier is more in number.

## n-type Semiconductor:

Majority carrier: Electrons

Minority carrier: Holes

Doped with atoms having 5 valence electrons (like phosphorus)

## p-type Semiconductor:

Majority carrier: Holes

Minority carrier: Electrons

Doped with atoms having 3 valence electrons (like boron)

In undoped semiconductor material at room temperature there are a number of free electrons and holes. These are due to thermal energy causing some electrons to break the bonds with their atoms and enter the conduction band. This process creates pairs of holes and electrons, and is appropriately termed hole-electron pair generation. The opposite effect, called recombination, occurs when an electron falls into a hole in the valence band. Because there are many more electrons than holes in n-type material, electrons are said to be the majority charge carriers, and holes are said to be minority carriers in n-type material. In p-type material, holes are the majority carriers and electrons are minority carriers.

Inside a semiconductor, a p-n junction is an interface or a border between two semiconductor material types, namely the p-type and the n-type.

## Formation of P-N Junction

When we utilize various semiconductor materials to form a p-n junction, there will be a grain boundary that will prevent electrons from moving from one side to the other by scattering electrons and holes, which is why we employ the doping procedure.

For example, Consider a p-type silicon semiconductor sheet that is very thin. A portion of the p-type Si will be changed to n-type silicon if a tiny quantity of pentavalent impurity is added. This sheet will now have both a p-type and an n-type area, as well as a junction between the two. Diffusion and drift are the two sorts of processes that occur following the creation of a p-n junction. As we all know, the concentration of holes and electrons on the two sides of a junction differs, with holes from the p-side diffusing to the n-side and electrons from the n-side diffusing to the p-side. This causes a diffusion current to flow across the connection.

## Real-Life Use Cases of Semiconductor Physics

- Smartphones and Computers

Semiconductor chips such as microprocessors, memory ICs, and transistors are the core components of smartphones, laptops, and computers.

- Electric Vehicles (EVs)

Power semiconductor devices like MOSFETs and IGBTs control battery charging, motor driving, regenerative braking, and energy management systems in EVs.

- Solar Energy Systems

Solar panels use semiconductor materials such as silicon to convert sunlight into electricity using the photovoltaic effect.

- LED Bulbs and Displays

LEDs used in home lighting, TVs, traffic lights, and digital displays are semiconductor-based light-emitting devices.

- Medical Equipment

Semiconductor sensors and ICs are used in ECG machines, MRI systems, digital thermometers, pulse oximeters, and wearable health devices.

- Mobile Charging Adapters and SMPS

Rectifier diodes, transistors, and switching devices are used in Switched Mode Power Supplies (SMPS) for efficient power conversion.

- Communication Systems

Semiconductor lasers, photodiodes, and RF transistors are used in fiber optic communication, Wi-Fi routers, and 5G networks.

- Home Automation and IoT Devices

Smart sensors based on semiconductor technology are used in smart homes, security systems, motion detectors, and automated appliances.

- Automotive Electronics

Modern vehicles use semiconductor devices in ABS systems, airbags, engine control units (ECUs), GPS navigation, and automatic climate control.

- Industrial Automation

Semiconductor-based controllers, PLCs, and sensors are used in robotics, manufacturing plants, and process automation industries.

- Consumer Electronics

Devices such as televisions, washing machines, refrigerators, and air conditioners rely on semiconductor ICs and control circuits.

- Banking and Security Systems

Semiconductor chips are used in ATM cards, biometric scanners, RFID tags, and digital payment systems for secure transactions.

- Satellite and Space Technology

Semiconductor components are used in satellites, radar systems, GPS technology, and spacecraft communication systems.

- Renewable Energy Hybrid Systems

In hybrid solar-wind systems integrated with EVs, semiconductor converters and controllers manage power flow, battery SOC, and grid interaction.

- Artificial Intelligence Hardware

AI processors, GPUs, and neural processing units (NPUs) are fabricated using advanced semiconductor technologies for machine learning and data processing.
