Theory:

The Kirchhoff Voltage Law KVL states that the algebraic sum of voltage produced and the voltage dropped in a closed loop (a closed path) of an electric circuit is always equal.

Learning outcomes:

Understand the Principle

Explain Kirchhoff’s Voltage Law and its basis in energy conservation.

Recognize that the sum of voltage drops around any closed loop in a circuit is zero.

Apply KVL to Electrical Circuits

Identify loops in a circuit and apply KVL to write voltage equations.

Solve for unknown voltages or currents using KVL equations.

Analyze Series and Complex Circuits

Use KVL in analyzing simple series circuits.

Combine KVL with Ohm’s Law to solve more complex circuits (e.g., series-parallel networks).

Verify Circuit Behavior

Check the correctness of circuit operation by verifying KVL.

Understand and troubleshoot real-world circuits using KVL.

Integrate with Other Circuit Laws

Apply KVL together with Kirchhoff’s Current Law (KCL) for full circuit analysis.

Understand its role in mesh analysis.

Result: We have verified KVL law.

### Slide 3
- Kirchhoff’s Laws
- Kirchhoff's Laws are the basic laws used in electrostatics to solve complex circuit questions.
- There are  two laws Kirchhoff’s Current Law and Kirchhoff’s Voltage Law.
- These laws deal with the flow of current and the voltage applied in the complex circuit and provide a way to solve these complex circuits.
- 2

### Slide 4
- Kirchhoff’s Voltage Law
- Kirchhoff’s Voltage Law states that the algebraic sum of the voltages (or voltage drops) in any closed path of a network that is transverse in a single direction is zero.
- Additional / Recommended Readings:
- https://circuitglobe.com/what-is-kirchhoffs-current-law-and-kirchhoffs-voltage-law.html
- Figure.1 KVL Law

### Slide 5
- Sign Conventions for KVL
- Take Positive sign for current entering the junction and negative for current leaving the junction
- In a loop, a rise in potential difference or EMF from lower to higher is always seen as positive i.e. moving from negative to positive terminal
- In a loop, a reduction in potential difference or EMF from higher to lower is always seen as negative i.e. moving from positive to negative terminal.
