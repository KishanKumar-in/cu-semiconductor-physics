Manual of Experiment:

Aim: To find the Voltage Current

relation in R-L series circuit and determine Power Factor.

Apparatus:

Resistor (R) – Known resistance value (in ohms)

Inductor (L) – Known inductance value (in henrys)

AC Power Supply – Typically low-voltage sinusoidal source

Connecting Wires – For circuit connections

Ammeter (AC) – To measure current in the circuit

Voltmeter (AC) – To measure voltage across resistor and inductor

Multimeter (optional) – For cross-checking measurements

Breadboard or Circuit Board – For assembling the components

Switch (optional) – To control the circuit powerTheory: A circuit that contains a pure resistance R ohms connected in series with a coil having a pure inductance of L (Henry) is known as RL Series Circuit. When an AC supply voltage V is applied, the current, I flows in the circuit.

Circuit Diagram:

Fig.1 Series RL Circuit

Observation Table:

Calculation:

Power Factor= P/V.I

Learning Outcomes:

Understand the Behavior of RL Circuits

Describe how current and voltage behave in a series RL circuit under AC supply.

Understand the concept of phase difference between voltage and current.

Measure and Analyze Circuit Parameters

Accurately measure voltage across the resistor and inductor using a voltmeter.

Calculate current using an ammeter or Ohm's Law.

Calculate Impedance and Phase Angle

Determine the total impedance of the RL circuit.

Calculate and interpret the phase angle between voltage and current.

Apply Theoretical Formulas to Practical Setup

Verify the relationship: V=(VR)2+(VL)2V = \sqrt{(VR)^2 + (VL)^2}​

Use vector diagrams (phasors) to analyze the circuit.

Relate to Real-World Applications

Understand how RL circuits are used in filters, timers, and inductive loads like motors.

Result: We have designed series RL circuit and have determined power factor of the circuit.
