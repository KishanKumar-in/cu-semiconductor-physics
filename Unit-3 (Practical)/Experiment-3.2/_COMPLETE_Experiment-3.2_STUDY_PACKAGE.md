Manual of Experiment:

Aim: To Verify Kirchhoff’s

current Law.

Apparatus:

Theory:  It is an application of the principle of conservation of electric charge. The law states that at any circuit junction, the sum of the currents flowing into and out of that junction are equal.

The sum of all currents entering a node is equal to the sum of all currents leaving the node.

Learning Outcome:

Understand the Principle

Explain Kirchhoff’s Current Law as a consequence of charge conservation.

State that the total current entering a junction equals the total current leaving it.

Identify Nodes in a Circuit

Recognize nodes (junction points) in electrical circuits where currents meet or split.

Apply KCL to Solve Circuits

Write current equations at nodes using KCL.

Calculate unknown branch currents using KCL equations.

Analyze Parallel and Complex Circuits

Use KCL in analyzing parallel circuits and node-based networks.

Combine KCL with Ohm’s Law for solving complex circuits.

Verify and Troubleshoot Circuits

Confirm the correctness of current distribution in a circuit.

Use KCL for diagnosing faults in electrical networks.

Integrate with KVL for Full Analysis

Use KCL along with Kirchhoff’s Voltage Law (KVL) in systematic circuit analysis methods like nodal analysis.

Result: We have verified KCL.

### Slide 3
- Kirchhoff’s Laws
- Kirchhoff's Laws are the basic laws used in electrostatics to solve complex circuit questions.
- There are  two laws Kirchhoff’s Current Law and Kirchhoff’s Voltage Law.
- These laws deal with the flow of current and the voltage applied in the complex circuit and provide a way to solve these complex circuits.
- 2

### Slide 4
- Kirchhoff’s Current Law
- Kirchhoff’s Current Law states that” the algebraic sum of all the currents at any node point or a junction of a circuit is zero”.
- Σ I = 0

### Slide 5
- Sign Conventions for KCL
- Take Positive sign for current entering the junction and negative for current leaving the junction.
- If the looping direction is the same as the current flowing through the circuit, the voltage drop across the resistor is considered negative.
